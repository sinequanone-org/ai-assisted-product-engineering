# Product discovery

Read the mission, user research, market requirements, product requirements, and
accepted decisions relevant to the request. If they are absent, create only the
records needed for the decision. Preserve the user's chosen audience and model.

Define who has the problem, what they do today, the cost or failure they face,
the outcome they need, and the constraints. Separate a user's need from a
proposed technical mechanism. State the strongest alternative, including keeping
the current process.

Label evidence as observation, reported experience, inference, or hypothesis.
Record source, date, population, and limits. A feasibility demo does not establish
demand. An assistant estimate does not establish market size or willingness to pay.

Before a study or experiment, define the question, population, method, success
measure, decision threshold, budget, and stopping condition. Include evidence
that could reject the preferred option. Do not conduct external outreach or spend
money without authorization.

For a material product choice, capture market need, alternatives, distribution,
economic assumptions, non-goals, and validation plan. Then define product
requirements with stable IDs, journeys, acceptance criteria, quality constraints,
dependencies, and unresolved choices. Link requirements to the need they serve.

Use explicit units and denominators for costs and benefits. Distinguish measured
cost from scenario cost. Include failure, retries, support, and recovery when
they materially affect the decision.

Recommend the smallest useful scope with a reason and a condition for revisiting
it. Record proposed, accepted, deferred, and rejected choices separately. Do not
invent user approval or delivery dates.

Output a decision-ready brief or update the existing MRD/PRD. Name the next
validation step and the evidence needed to move from hypothesis to delivery.
